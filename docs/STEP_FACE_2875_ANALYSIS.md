# STEP face #2875 trim and triangulation investigation

**Status: bounded research only.** The work below diagnoses one face in the
ESP8266 model-16 STEP asset. It does not repair or export geometry, qualify a
complete solid, or enable production STEP admission.

## Pinned source and topology

The materialized dataset source is
`data/board-sources/esp8266-nodemcu-reference/structured-source/embedded-models/16.step`
from the Tokito app dataset's ESP8266 source candidate. Its SHA-256 is
`7234dff19581f670dd0f65f1b3c5d76abb6054004ffea8627bf0ef505f66138f`.

The inspected chain is `ADVANCED_FACE #2875` → rational B-spline surface
`#10410` → outer bound `#8995` → edge loop `#8007`. The surface has degree 2×9,
a 3×18 control net, and a closed V parameter with period approximately
`1.40496092306096`. Its V knots are approximately 0, 1.052954291263 and
1.404960923061, with multiplicities 10, 8 and 10. The degree-9 internal knot
therefore has C1 continuity under the standard degree-minus-multiplicity rule;
this identifies a place requiring focused sampling, but does not prove the
cause of the observed mesh defects. The loop has four oriented B-spline edge curves
(`#2809`, `#470`, `#7109`, `#4933`). The source stores 3D edge curves; the
inspected face path has no corresponding explicit UV trim curves.

## Rust trim candidate and independent quality checks

The diagnostic now builds a bounded *research candidate* from the sampled UV
loop using simple-polygon ear clipping and three rounds of uniform subdivision.
It contains 3,393 UV vertices and 6,016 triangles; sampled chord error is
`0.004714` source units against a `0.005` threshold. The pinned E2E requires
this candidate and its convergence result. This changes no reader mesh, does
not produce an export, and does not set qualification or admission.

FreeCAD maps all candidate vertices onto the same underlying B-spline face with
maximum Rust-to-FreeCAD surface-point residual `3.30e-15` model units. At
`1e-7 mm`, 3,293 of 3,393 candidate vertices classify inside the trimmed face;
100 classify outside. Separately, all 6,016 triangle UV centroids classify
inside, and the 100 sampled source-boundary points lie on the face boundary.
Passing centroid containment does not prove that every mesh vertex or edge is
inside the trim. The maximum straight-triangle centroid distance to the trimmed
face is `0.002669 mm` (RMS `0.000737 mm`), but that positional measurement alone
does not establish correct normals or area.

The independent normal comparison rejects the candidate: `1,291 / 6,016`
triangles have nonpositive alignment with the oriented FreeCAD face normal
(minimum `-0.999992`). All UV triangles have positive winding. A separate
finite-difference estimate of `dS/du × dS/dv` agrees with FreeCAD's face-normal
test at aggregate and 8×8-cell count level; per-triangle sign correlation was
not recorded, so this does not establish pointwise agreement. Uniform
refinement reduces sampled chord error and brings the summed triangle area
closer to the FreeCAD face area, but does not close the normal gap. At 24,064 /
96,256 / 385,024 triangles, respectively, 3,995 / 11,905 / 42,336 remain
nonpositive (16.6% / 12.4% / 11.0%); area ratios are 1.383× / 1.156× / 1.064×.
At 385,024 triangles, maximum sampled centroid chord error is `0.0000657 mm`,
yet minimum normal alignment remains `-0.99999985`. This experiment does not
identify whether the remaining discrepancy comes from local parameterization,
branch choice, or a high-curvature/singular region.

Vertex containment, face-normal orientation, area convergence, and complete
solid validation therefore remain failed qualification gates. The all-inside
centroid result cannot clear the 100 outside vertices.

Do not flip triangles or treat this candidate as a repaired face. The next
investigation is continuity-aware UV branch selection per ordered source edge,
followed by adaptive angular as well as chordal refinement. Acceptance needs
consistent oriented normals, trim/loop and periodic-seam continuity, bounded
surface deviation, area convergence, and complete-solid checks against the
independent reference. Open CASCADE describes face normal direction from
`dS/du × dS/dv` with the face orientation controlling the sign; its meshing
release notes also distinguish linear and angular deflection controls.

## Regression and remaining gate

`experiments/step-evaluation/tests/model16_source_face_trim_e2e.py` pins the
source hash and verifies the diagnostic subprocess's topology, residuals,
pre-allocation curve-storage budget, expected candidate counts, convergence,
chord bound, refusal status and no-export behavior. The Rust polygon predicate
uses separate area and coordinate tolerances and has a scale-invariance
regression. When the ESP8266 source set is passed to `scripts/verify-local.ps1`,
this E2E check runs automatically.

The next geometry task is to sample each ordered 3D source edge adaptively,
project samples while preserving continuity of the V-period branch, and measure
the residual and branch changes at the closed seam and near the internal knot.
Then build an original Rust trim pipeline that produces a valid, nondegenerate,
consistently oriented mesh. Compare it against FreeCAD for the full face and
solid, including boundary and vertex containment, surface deviation, triangle
winding and topology. Passing this one face test would still not establish
STEP/library parity or qualify other board models. Production admission remains
disabled.

Primary references: [Open CASCADE topology training](https://dev.opencascade.org/sites/default/files/pdf/Topology.pdf)
for surface-normal direction and face orientation, and [Open CASCADE 7.4
release notes](https://dev.opencascade.org/sites/default/files/documents/release_notes_7.4.0.pdf)
for linear and angular deflection controls in BRepMesh.
